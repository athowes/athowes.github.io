Inference for a complex model of HIV indicators: informal discussion
The Paper Background
* Publication Context: PhD work published in the Journal of Theoretical Biology special edition on biological inference
* The Naomi Model: A spatial evidence synthesis model used to produce district-level HIV epidemic indicators across sub-Saharan Africa, supporting over 35 countries in generating estimates during annual workshops facilitated by UNAIDS
* The Problem: Naomi comprises multiple linked generalized linear mixed models with hundreds of parameters and over 20 hyperparameters—more than typical methods like INLA can handle effectively
* Technical Challenge: The model's scale and complex posterior geometry make MCMC approaches prohibitively slow for interactive use during country workshops, while existing empirical Bayes approaches using TMB don't account for hyperparameter uncertainty
What the Paper Contributes
* Novel PCA-AGHQ Method: Developed an extension of adaptive Gauss-Hermite quadrature using principal components analysis to handle integration over many hyperparameters. Making inference feasible for models with 20+ hyperparameters that would otherwise require exponentially many nodes
* Practical Implementation: Built on existing TMB infrastructure, making the method compatible with any model using TMB templates (including the existing Naomi implementation)
* Demonstrated Parameter Improvements: In the Malawi case study, showed 26% reduction in root mean square error for latent field posterior means and 70% reduction for posterior standard deviations compared to empirical Bayes
Limitations of the Paper and Opportunities for Future Work
Technical Shortcomings and Mixed Results
* Limited Downstream Impact: While parameter estimation improved significantly, translation to better HIV indicator estimates was more limited. The root cause of what ultimately matters for public health decisions
* Systematic Inaccuracies Remain: Both our method and empirical Bayes showed substantial errors compared to gold-standard MCMC for key policy-relevant outputs like exceedance probabilities (e.g., determining if districts meet 90% ART coverage targets)
* Scale-Dependent Coverage Issues: PCA-AGHQ nodes provided good coverage for some hyperparameters but concentrated at the mode for others, particularly affecting parameters on logarithmic vs. logistic scales
* Implementation Gaps: Software implementation could be significantly stronger and more accessible to practitioners
Specific Areas for Future Development
* Quadrature Grid Improvements: Better approaches for allocating computational effort across dimensions, potentially using sensitivity analysis measures like Shapley values rather than simple PCA variance explanation
* Cross-Scale Integration: Methods to better handle hyperparameters on different scales (logit vs. log) that currently receive unequal treatment in the PCA decomposition
* Scalability Questions: While the method works for Malawi (30 districts), larger countries with hundreds of districts may still require further approximations
* Beyond TMB: Exploring implementation in other frameworks like NIMBLE or Stan could provide access to additional algorithmic options
Connections of the Paper to Current Work on Rt
Methodological Parallels
* Current Application: Using mgcv to fit GAMs for effective reproduction number of respiratory infections in US counties
* Comparative Context: Contrasting with older EpiNow2 model (state-level, MCMC-fitted) reveals similar methodological trade-offs between approximation methods and exact but computationally impractical inference
* Recurring Themes: The same fundamental tension appears across different disease modeling contexts: balancing theoretical rigor with practical computational constraints
Future Potential
* Scaling Challenges: Current mgcv approach works well for single-source data, but integrating multiple evidence sources would require methods beyond mgcv
* Framework Possibilities: Template Model Builder (TMB) could be a candidate framework for more complex evidence synthesis, making the HIV modeling inference approaches relevant again
* Cross-Application Learning: Lessons from HIV indicator estimation inform current respiratory disease modeling and vice versa
Broader Reflections About the Field
The Models vs. Data Fundamental Tension
* Sophistication Limits: Evidence synthesis models can be highly sophisticated, but their utility is fundamentally constrained by the quality and availability of underlying data
* Data Instability: Data inputs (Demographic and Health Surveys) can shift rapidly due to external circumstances, including political instability in regions where monitoring is most critical
* Resource Allocation Dilemma: This raises a fundamental question: whether to invest in increasingly complex models or prioritize stronger, more consistent data collection infrastructure
* Beyond Academia: This tension has direct implications for public health organizations such as the CDC, which face similar choices about where to focus limited analytical resources
Software as the Foundation for Methods Development
* Methods Enabled by Software: Good software doesn't just implement methods—it enables methodological innovation by making research easier, faster, and more accessible
* Design Trade-offs: Different software philosophies make different trade-offs—R-INLA's highly specialized approach offers computational power but can present accessibility challenges for broader adoption
* Software Quality Multiplies Impact: Well-designed, accessible software amplifies methodological contributions by lowering barriers for other researchers to build upon, test, and extend the work
* Building on Foundations: Software development benefits from building on existing, well-maintained tools, allowing researchers to focus unique effort where genuine value can be added
* Implementation as Innovation Enabler: Strong software implementation doesn't just disseminate methods—it creates the foundation for future methodological advances by other researchers
Research Reality and Progress
* Imperfection as Normal: The paper represents both achievement and imperfection—and recognizing limitations doesn't diminish its value as a meaningful milestone
* Trade-offs Are Inevitable: Impactful research involves navigating tensions between theoretical elegance and practical utility, between reproducible ideals and implementable reality
* Incremental Value: Modest advances that connect to ongoing methodological conversations can be more valuable than dramatic but isolated breakthroughs
* Honest Assessment: Professional constraints limit further development opportunities, but the research direction remains compelling and the lessons learned continue to inform current work
The Reproducibility Reality Check
* The Award Paradox: Despite winning a reproducibility award, the work is not fully reproducible in practice—highlighting how recognition can mask underlying challenges
* Infrastructure Dependencies: Reproducibility requires not just code availability but stable computational environments, package versions, and hardware configurations that proved harder to maintain than anticipated
* Beyond Good Intentions: Even with deliberate effort and best practices, the gap between reproducibility ideals and implementation reality remains substantial
* Learning Through Doing: Sometimes the most valuable research outcomes are insights about the research process itself—understanding these challenges firsthand provides crucial perspective for future work
The Bigger Picture
* Public Health Impact: Methodological improvements in biological inference ultimately serve public health decision-making, making the connection between academic research and practical application crucial
* Collaborative Science: The most effective research acknowledges its limitations while contributing meaningfully to larger scientific conversations
* Learning Through Doing: Sometimes the most valuable outcomes are the insights gained about research process, software development, and the practical challenges of doing rigorous, reproducible work
